You’re exploring an AI-only game engine for serious creators working alone or in teams of 1–5. The thesis is that AI will let one or two people realize an ambitious artistic vision with far less production overhead. The interface would mainly show the game and an AI control window, with very few buttons; agents would handle coding, meshes, scene changes, debugging, testing, and publishing. You may build your own tightly integrated agent harness. You want publicly readable source, restrictions on copying or redistributing the engine, and royalties from commercial games—which points to a **source-available license**, rather than open source. Godot, OpenCode, Three.js, and other permissively licensed projects are potential building blocks.

The main unresolved choice is the language and runtime. You prefer simplicity and ideally one primary language, balancing AI effectiveness, fast feedback, and enough performance for ambitious 3D games on an M1 MacBook Air. TypeScript with Bun or another desktop runtime remains a plausible option; browser support isn’t required, and GPU shader languages are allowed. C++ provides more performance control but makes iteration harder; C# offers additional optimization options without automatically delivering much better performance than well-written TypeScript. We haven’t chosen a stack or demonstrated GTA/Zelda-scale performance: the key takeaway is that engine architecture, efficient simulation, and preserving the game’s state between edits matter alongside language choice.
